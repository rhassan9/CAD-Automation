2. CAD Drafting and Construction Print Standards  

--- PAGE 3 ---
Visual clarity and drafting precision directly impact municipal permit approval speed and 
field safety. Designers shall not deviate from the following standards to ensure all prints are 
professional, legible, and constructible.  
Layer and Annotation Rigor  
• Text Mandate:  All text—including street names, callouts, and addresses —shall be 
in ALL CAPS . 
• Abbreviations:  Street abbreviations shall not include periods (e.g., use "DR" not 
"DR.").  
• Street Names:  Must be CENTERED  between Edge of Pavement (EOP) lines and 
aligned parallel to the EOP.  
• Background Masks:  Mandated for all addresses, footage labels, and sheet titles to 
ensure legibility.  
• Linetype Synchronization:  The font size for Edge of Pavement (EOP) and Right of 
Way (ROW) linetypes must be identical.  
• Linework Integrity:  Parcel lines shall be trimmed to the ROW; EOP corners must be 
filleted; utility lines shall be turned off on construction prints.  
• Pole Attachments:  All poles must show the owner, Pole ID, Lat/Long, and Lumos 
approval status.

Conduit Placement and Road Bore Logic  
• The Perpendicular Mandate:  All road bores shall be drawn at 90-degree angles . 
Angled bores are strictly prohibited.  
• Pull Box Spacing:  Maximum footage between pull boxes is 650 feet. 
• Drop Capacity:  Maximum of 6 drops per conduit run. If exceeded, a second conduit 
is required.  
• Exclusion Zones:  Infrastructure shall never be placed in brick pavers or "Downtown 
Business Districts" where drops would require destroying concrete or blowing out 
the front of businesses.  
• Property Lines:  Handholes and flower pots shall be placed on property lines to 
minimize parcel obstruction.  
Slack and Maintenance Loop Standards  
• Splitter HH:  15-foot loop per splitter (Cyan).  
• HH Splice Locations:  50-foot loop at each 3 -way splice location (Cyan).  
• Aerial Coils:  200-foot storage loops every 1,500 –2,000 feet minimum.  
• Transitions:  100-foot aerial coil at riser poles (aerial to buried).  
• Fiber Markers:  Placed every 500 feet following the maintenance slack guideline, 
not at every handhole.

4. Fiber Architecture and Splicing Standards  
--- PAGE 6 ---
The "Path of Light" philosophy dictates that "Fixed Position" logic is non -negotiable. Shifting 
counts to fill gaps is prohibited.  
The Splitter Hierarchy and Naming  
A single XGSPON port services 32 customers.  
• Naming Syntax:  [NODE]_[FEEDER]_[LEVEL][PORT]_[CITY CLLI][STATE]  (e.g., 
OLT11_3P_7S_FTMYFL ). 
• IT Stack Requirements:  The 4-character City CLLI and 2 -character State identifier 
are mandatory for database synchronization.  
• Ring Fiber Logic:  Named using the 12 IN / 12 OUT  convention (e.g., LE1_OLT07A: 1 -
12). 
The "No Indexing" and "XD" Mandate  
Fibers shall remain in the exact color position they occupied prior to the splice.  
• Rule: Utilize the highest non -split feeder counts first  (e.g., fiber 176 used before 
fiber 1).  
• XD Notation:  Utilized counts are replaced with "XD" dead count notations to 
maintain strand alignment.  
• Color-for-Color Exception:  If a new cable is smaller than the incoming count (e.g., 
placing fiber 49 into a 48 -count cable), fiber 49 (a "blue" sequence fiber) shall be 
placed in the Blue position (Position 1)  to maintain color alignment.  
Feeder and Distribution Separation  
• 80% Capacity Rule:  Upsize cable once 80% capacity is reached.  
• Separation:  Distribution fibers must come after non-split feeders in the tube.  
• Exception:  Front-loading distribution fibers is only permitted if utilizing the very last 
non-split feeder fiber  in the cable.  
 
Fiber Callouts  
A. Introduction to Fiber Documentation Standards  
Core Objectives  The Lumos drafting standard is designed to achieve three non -negotiable 
goals:  
--- PAGE 7 ---
• Accuracy in Strand Alignment:  Every fiber must be accounted for and positioned 
precisely within its specific tube or ribbon.  
• Adherence to "Color for Color" Logic:  Maintaining a direct relationship between a 
fiber’s assignment number and its physical color position to ensure field 
technicians can identify strands without guesswork.  
• Hierarchical Differentiation:  Establishing a clear visual and nomenclatural 
distinction between feeder fibers (transporting light from the cabinet) and 
distribution fibers (serving the individual address).  
B. The Anatomy of a Lumos Fiber Callout  
The structural formatting of a Lumos fiber callout is a technical requirement, not a stylistic 
preference. Technicians in the field rely on the absolute consistency of these annotations 
to perform complex splices under varied conditions.

• Punctuation Requirements:  Designers MUST use commas to separate specific

• The "Same Line" Rule:  To ensure data integrity, the Cable Name and the first

• The 20% Spare Mandate:  Every cable must represent at least 20% spare capacity  
utilizing the "XD" count. This ensures future -proofing and limits the need for 
disruptive future overlays.  
C. Hierarchical Naming and Splitter Identification  
Hierarchical naming is the only way to maintain the "Path of Light" traceability from the 
Optical Line Terminal (OLT) to the customer’s residence. This logic allows a maintenance 
technician to trace a failure at a specific address back through the secondar y and primary 
splitters to the exact feeder fiber at the cabinet.  
Deconstructing the Naming Logic  
Consider the secondary splitter string: OLT11_3P_7S_FTMYFL, 25 -28 
1. Node (OLT11):  The specific field node or CO site.  
2. Feeder Fiber Assignment (3):  The specific feeder fiber (port) arriving from the 
cabinet.  
3. Splitter Type (P/S):  Identifies the hierarchy level (P = Primary 1x8, S = Secondary 
1x4).  
4. Port Assignment (7):  The specific port off the primary splitter feeding this 
secondary unit.  
5. CLLI Code/State (FTMYFL):  The 4-character city CLLI and 2 -character state 
identifier.  
6. Output Range (25 -28): The distribution fibers serving the individual addresses.  
D. The "No Indexing" Mandate and the "XD" Notation  
Lumos strictly enforces a "No Indexing"  policy. Fiber counts must never be moved up a 
tube or ribbon at a splice point to fill empty positions. Indexing leads to "wasteful splicing" 
and breaks the color -coding logic required for field operations.  (note exception below)  
The Role of "XD" Notation  
"XD" notation is the placeholder for spare, reserved, or utilized counts. Its purpose is to 
maintain fixed-position fiber indexing . When a fiber is utilized at a splitter, it is replaced 
with "XD" in the outgoing callout to keep the remaining fibers in their original physical color 
positions.

E. Splicing Order of Operations and Technical Exceptions  
Standardized sequencing is required for constructible prints. Lumos mandates that Non-
split Feeder fibers must be placed before Distribution (Split) fibers  in any callout.  
The "Color for Color" Exception (Wrap -around Logic)  
The "No Indexing" rule is only superseded when a new cable is physically too small to hold 
original assignment numbers. For example, if fiber 49 is being spliced into a 48 -count 
cable, the physical position 49 does not exist. Because fiber 49 is inherently  the first fiber of 
the next 48 -count set (a "blue" fiber), it must be placed in the first available blue position 
(Position 1) . This wrap -around is triggered solely because the physical position cannot be 
utilized.  
The Front -Loading Exception and Cable Sizing  
Distribution fibers may only be front-loaded (placed at the start of a cable) if the very last 
non-split feeder fiber  in that cable has been utilized.  
F. Visual Implementation: Splice Boxes and Drafting Standards  
Splice Point (SP) boxes are the visual instructions for construction crews. They must be 
drafted with absolute clarity.  
"IN" vs. "OUT" Structure and Summation  
• IN: Identifies incoming cable names, counts, and assignments.  
--- PAGE 10 ---
• OUT: Identifies all outgoing cables and splitter tails.  
• Mandatory Calculation:  Designers MUST verify that the sum of outgoing fibers 
accounts for all incoming strands , including XD spares.  
CAD Drafting Checklist  
• Orientation:  All callouts MUST be perfectly horizontal.  
• Background Masks:  Mandatory for all text to ensure legibility over map features.  
• Leaders:  Must snap to the closest corner of the splitter or HH block.  
• Case: ALL CAPS for all text.  
• Indentation Rule:  If a data string for a single cable overflows to a second line, the 
"subset child line" must be indented to show it belongs to the cable listed above.  
• Footage Notation:  Use H-B (Handhole to Bend), B-F (Bore Pit to Flowerpot), or B-H 
(Bore Pit to Handhole). The "B" notation for a Bore Pit is critical for calculating labor 
costs in the Labor Span Sheet.

. Quality Control and Submission Protocol  
The professional appearance of a print is a proxy for technical accuracy. Designers shall 
perform a self-17-point QC review sequence  before submission : 
1. Static Viewport Check:  Scroll all pages; no entities shall shift.  
2. Title Page:  Verify Lat/Long (second value must be negative), Legend, and Revision 
Table.  
3. Sheet Index:  Verify order and ensure Wipeout frames are turned OFF . 
4. Mini Map:  Consistency check across all sheets.  
5. Matchlines:  Accuracy, alignment, and centering on streets.  
6. Street Names:  Correct spelling, centering, and no periods  in abbreviations.  

--- PAGE 12 ---
7. Linework:  Verify EOP corners are filleted and ROW is trimmed.  
8. Dimensions:  Parallelism and snap accuracy; no overlaps.  
9. Addresses:  Readability, background masks, and no overlap.  
10. Blocks: All blocks brought to front and oriented correctly.  
11. Callouts:  Horizontal alignment and correct address/street spelling.  
12. Footages:  Correct abbreviations (H -B, B-F) and background masks.  
13. TO CO Arrows:  Required on every page containing FOC.  
14. North Arrow:  Required on every print sheet.  
15. VL/C Notations:  "VL" for vacant lots and "C" for commercial properties.  
16. Hatching:  Verify Gray (NN) or Yellow (Private) hatches; no hatching on public roads.  
17. Design Efficiency:  Verify maximum 6 drops per run and no T -connections in 
conduit.  
The designer holds final responsibility for providing an efficient, professional, and 
constructible design. There is no excuse for non -compliance.
